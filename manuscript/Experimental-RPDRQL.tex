\section{EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate the four security quantities defined in Section~\ref{subsec-security-objectives} at two levels. Controlled experiments exercise the bounded belief update, semantic authorization rule, post-attack correction, and policy-promotion constraint directly. Feeder simulations then place the same knowledge mechanisms in a restoration loop with an AC power-flow verifier. Feeder statistics are computed from per-seed simulator logs; the legacy plotting arrays are not used in the reported results.

\subsection{Evaluation Setup and Metrics}
\label{subsec-exp-questions}

The controlled experiments report belief distortion $D_B$, false semantic-authorization probability, correction time $T_{\rm corr}$, and deployed-policy distance $D_\pi$. The feeder experiments additionally report the normalized area under the restored-service curve (AURC), time to 90\% service restoration $T_{\rm crit}$, fallback frequency $r_{\rm fb}$, and mean authorization-loop latency $T_{\rm gate}$.

For the feeder results, $P_{\rm FA}$ denotes the \emph{semantic false-authorization rate}. A candidate contributes to this rate when it is released by the method's semantic eligibility rule but its ground-truth runtime availability prerequisite is false. This event is scored before AC verification. In the simulator, an unavailable prerequisite causes that candidate attempt to fail before physical verification and consumes one of the bounded candidate attempts; the ground-truth value is not supplied to the belief updater, policy, or candidate ranking. Thus $P_{\rm FA}$ measures an error in semantic authority, not an executed invariant-constraint violation.

The feeder attack budget is $\Omega_A=(f_p,B_z,H_A,\epsilon_s)$. We use none $(0,0,0,0)$, low $(1,2,4,0.01)$, medium $(2,6,8,0.02)$, and high $(3,12,12,0.05)$. During the bounded attack interval, the evidence generator cycles through fabrication, replay, delay, missing-evidence, and correlated-source modes. The protected actuation path and the AC verifier are outside this attack interface.

\subsection{Belief Distortion and Post-Attack Correction}
\label{subsec-exp-belief}

For the belief experiment, attacked and reference trajectories receive identical uncompromised provenance-group contributions. Each compromised group contributes a per-update difference in $[0,2\beta_{\max}]$. We run 800 independent 80-update trials for each $f_p$ and record the maximum belief deviation.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/kgfedmarl_belief_budget.eps}
\caption{Controlled belief distortion versus the number of compromised provenance groups. Mean and 95th-percentile deviations are compared with the sufficient bound in Theorem~\ref{thm-bounded-poisoning}.}
\label{fig-exp-belief-budget}
\end{figure}

Mean $D_B$ rises from 0.0543 at $f_p=1$ to 0.2208 at $f_p=5$; the corresponding 95th percentiles are 0.0605 and 0.2339. The sufficient bounds are 0.0833 and 0.4167. The observed deviations stay below the envelope for all tested $f_p$. The gap widens because the theorem allows every compromised group to take its worst signed contribution at every update, whereas the experiment samples contributions inside the admissible interval.

The correction experiment applies the worst signed group difference for $H_A$ updates and then removes the attack. From that point, attacked and reference processes receive identical effective uncompromised evidence, matching the sufficient condition of Theorem~\ref{thm-finite-correction}.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/kgfedmarl_correction_time.eps}
\caption{Post-attack correction time versus poisoning duration under the controlled worst signed group difference.}
\label{fig-exp-correction}
\end{figure}

Correction takes 6 updates after a two-update attack and 13 updates for the longest tested attacks. The saturation is consistent with the finite geometric accumulation in Eq.~\ref{eq-attack-duration-deviation}. The forgetting factor is fixed at $\lambda_f=0.85$ in this experiment; its effect on the poisoning envelope and correction time follows from Eqs.~\ref{eq-thm-belief-bound} and~\ref{eq-thm-correction-time}, rather than from a $\lambda_f$ sweep in Fig.~\ref{fig-exp-correction}.

\subsection{Semantic Authorization and Policy Propagation}
\label{subsec-exp-auth-policy}

The authorization experiment uses four evidence-dependent prerequisites. We set the calibration error to $\epsilon_q=0.005$ per prerequisite and sweep the aggregate semantic budget $\bar\delta_B$ from 0.01 to 0.10 with equal allocation. Each point uses 200,000 Monte Carlo actions. Independence is used by the generator only; the union bound in Theorem~\ref{thm-false-authorization} does not require independent prerequisites.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/kgfedmarl_false_authorization.eps}
\caption{Controlled false semantic-authorization probability versus aggregate semantic risk budget.}
\label{fig-exp-false-auth}
\end{figure}

The observed probability ranges from 0.0297 to 0.1148, while the sufficient bound $\bar\delta_B+\sum_q\epsilon_q$ ranges from 0.03 to 0.12. This experiment checks the calibration-to-gate relation used in Theorem~\ref{thm-false-authorization}. Invariant physical feasibility is evaluated separately by the authoritative verifier in the feeder loop.

For policy propagation, the attacked and reference deployments start from the same four-action policy on 64 fixed audit states and undergo up to ten successful promotions. Candidate updates satisfy $\kappa_\pi(0.55)=0.00215$ for the lower-trust attacked stream and $\kappa_\pi(0.90)=0.0032$ for the reference stream.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/kgfedmarl_policy_drift.eps}
\caption{Controlled deployed-policy drift on a fixed audit distribution.}
\label{fig-exp-policy-drift}
\end{figure}

After ten promotions, the attacked-versus-reference policy distance is 0.1379; the cumulative sufficient bound is 0.7279. The bound is loose because it combines Pinsker's inequality with a triangle inequality over promotion events and does not assume aligned update directions.

\subsection{Feeder-Level End-to-End Evaluation}
\label{subsec-exp-feeder}

The supplied environment provides the IEEE 33-bus and a 69-bus radial benchmark through pandapower. The larger cases use the canonical IEEE 123 and 8500-node OpenDSS feeders at a pinned upstream revision. The simulator exposes 132 buses for IEEE 123 and 4,876 buses for the 8500-node model. Loads are divided into 8, 10, 12, and 32 restoration blocks, with 8, 10, 12, and 16 recovery agents, respectively. Every physically evaluated candidate is solved by the authoritative AC verifier and accepted only if all monitored service nodes remain in $[0.90,1.10]$ p.u. For the OpenDSS cases, the service-node monitor set is fixed from the canonical solved base case. Ten internal or auxiliary nodes of the 8500-node case with canonical values near 0.06 p.u. are excluded from this service-voltage set.

The 8500-node damaged state is constructed from the canonical feasible operating point. Balanced load blocks are shed one at a time, and only verifier-feasible intermediate states are retained until the served-load ratio is about 34.3\%. During restoration, at most $K_{\max}=8$ ranked candidates are considered in each decision step. Each reported configuration uses 20 independent seeds.

All baseline methods use the same load-priority candidate policy and AC verifier. \emph{Oracle-AC} ranks only candidates whose current runtime availability is true and serves as an information upper reference. \emph{Latest-Evidence} uses the most recent aggregated evidence. \emph{Static-KG} keeps the initial availability state. RK-Guard uses the provenance-aware belief and semantic gate, with the same downstream candidate search and physical verifier.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/final_feeder_results/feeder_end_to_end_baseline.eps}
\caption{End-to-end restoration AURC on IEEE 33-bus under medium mixed recovery-knowledge poisoning. Bars show mean $\pm$ standard deviation over 20 seeds.}
\label{fig-feeder-baseline}
\end{figure}

RK-Guard obtains an AURC of $0.9150\pm0.0177$, compared with $0.9236\pm0.0102$ for Oracle-AC, $0.9139\pm0.0096$ for Latest-Evidence, and $0.9236\pm0.0102$ for Static-KG. The RK-Guard mean is 0.92\% below the oracle-information reference. Its semantic false-authorization rate is 0.0286, versus 0.1143 for Latest-Evidence and 0.2676 for Static-KG; these correspond to relative reductions of 75.0\% and 89.3\%. Latest-Evidence also has a larger mean $D_\pi$ (0.0332 versus 0.0126).

Static-KG reaches the same mean AURC as Oracle-AC in this setup despite its much larger $P_{\rm FA}$. The reason is specific to the simulator: an authorized candidate with an unavailable runtime prerequisite is recorded as a semantic false authorization, consumes a candidate attempt, and fails before AC verification; the bounded search may then try another candidate in the same decision step. The AURC comparison should therefore be read together with $P_{\rm FA}$ rather than as evidence that the static knowledge state is equally trustworthy.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/final_feeder_results/feeder_attack_intensity.eps}
\caption{Feeder-level belief distortion on IEEE 33-bus as the recovery-knowledge attack budget increases.}
\label{fig-feeder-intensity}
\end{figure}

Across none, low, medium, and high attack settings, mean $D_B$ is approximately 0, 0.1287, 0.1974, and 0.4303. The corresponding semantic false-authorization rates are 0, 0.0167, 0.0286, and 0.0962. AURC stays between 0.914 and 0.918. These results establish a clear change in the knowledge and authorization metrics over the tested attack budgets; the physical-service metric changes much less in the same runs. The experiment does not isolate which downstream safeguard is responsible for that difference.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/final_feeder_results/feeder_security_ablation.eps}
\caption{Security-boundary ablation on IEEE 33-bus under medium poisoning. The plotted quantity is the semantic false-authorization rate.}
\label{fig-feeder-ablation}
\end{figure}

The one-at-a-time ablations are consistent with the intended roles of the security mechanisms. Removing provenance-group caps raises mean $D_B$ from 0.1974 to 0.3708 (87.9\%) and $D_\pi$ from 0.0126 to 0.0305. With forgetting disabled, $D_B$ is 0.2562, $P_{\rm FA}$ is 0.0990, and $D_\pi$ is 0.0308. Removing the semantic gate raises $P_{\rm FA}$ to 0.2676, 9.4 times the full method, while AURC rises to 0.9236. Removing transition quarantine leaves the immediate belief and authorization metrics unchanged in this setup but increases $D_\pi$ from 0.0126 to 0.0164 (30.6\%). The fixed-radius variant is close to the full method over the 24-step feeder horizon ($D_\pi=0.01274$ versus $0.01257$). This feeder run is therefore insufficient to quantify the benefit of the trust-dependent promotion radius; a dedicated longer-horizon radius ablation would be needed for that claim.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/final_feeder_results/feeder_scalability.eps}
\caption{Authorization-loop latency across the four feeder cases under medium poisoning. Error bars show standard deviation over 20 seeds.}
\label{fig-feeder-scale}
\end{figure}

Mean authorization-loop latency is $10.92\pm3.99$ ms on IEEE 33-bus, $12.31\pm2.83$ ms on the supplied 69-bus case, $1.07\pm0.18$ ms on the IEEE 123 OpenDSS case, and $62.43\pm3.16$ ms on the 4,876-bus realization of the IEEE 8500-node feeder. The non-monotone values coincide with the use of different solver backends, so Fig.~\ref{fig-feeder-scale} is a cross-feeder runtime measurement rather than a pure algorithmic complexity curve.

For the 8500-node case, all 20 runs start from a verifier-feasible state with about 34.3\% served load, reach 90\% service at step 18, and finish at full served load. AURC is $0.7127\pm0.0009$ and the mean fallback rate is 0.0063. Relative to the earlier liveness-stress configuration, the present setup changes three items together: it uses 32 finer load blocks, constructs the damaged state along a verifier-feasible shedding path, and searches up to $K_{\max}=8$ ranked candidates. The current experiment shows that this revised configuration restores liveness under the same monitored $[0.90,1.10]$ p.u. voltage criterion; it does not identify the individual contribution of the three changes.
