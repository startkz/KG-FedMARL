\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{
\begin{abstract}
\justifying{Resilient restoration of a power cyber-physical system (PCPS) depends not only on electrical feasibility, but also on runtime knowledge of controller availability, measurement-path trust, communication reachability, authorization conditions, and previous recovery outcomes. During an attack, this knowledge may be missing, stale, conflicting, replayed, or deliberately fabricated. Existing restoration and knowledge-graph-assisted methods generally treat recovery knowledge as trusted side information or do not explicitly bound how corrupted evidence propagates into action authorization and subsequent online learning. We formulate this attack surface as \emph{recovery knowledge poisoning} and propose KG-FedMARL, a trustworthy online restoration framework that limits such propagation. KG-FedMARL separates a trusted semantic basis from uncertain runtime assertions and introduces three coupled mechanisms: a provenance-aware temporal belief update that bounds the influence of individual and correlated evidence sources; a risk-aware recovery gate that checks evidence-dependent prerequisites probabilistically while reserving invariant safety authorization for an authoritative physical verifier; and trust-gated online policy adaptation that quarantines uncertain transitions and constrains stable--candidate policy updates. Restoration is modeled as a constrained decentralized partially observable Markov decision process with four security objectives: bounded belief distortion, bounded false authorization, finite correction of poisoned beliefs after trustworthy evidence resumes, and bounded deployed-policy deviation. We establish mechanism-level guarantees that connect the adversarial evidence budget and framework parameters to these four quantities, thereby characterizing how far corrupted recovery knowledge can propagate through the recovery loop. The framework remains compatible with decentralized multi-agent reinforcement learning and federated deployment, but neither is assumed as part of the security root. Controlled mechanism-level experiments evaluate the four security quantities and show that belief distortion, false-authorization probability, correction time, and deployed-policy drift remain within the derived sufficient envelopes under bounded poisoning; a feeder-level evaluation protocol is additionally specified for end-to-end physical recovery under fabrication, replay, delay, missing evidence, and correlated-source poisoning.}
\end{abstract}
