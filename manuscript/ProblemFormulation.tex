\section{PROBLEM FORMULATION}
\label{sec-problem-formulation}
This section formalizes resilient restoration under adversarial recovery knowledge. The formulation separates partial observability, evidence-dependent action admissibility, worst-case recovery performance, and the security objective of bounding the propagation of erroneous knowledge. The attacker is modeled through the observation and evidence processes defined in Section~\ref{sec-system-model}, not as a cooperative decision agent.

\subsection{Constrained Dec-POMDP under Adversarial Knowledge}
\label{subsec-decpomdp}
Let $\mathcal{I}=\{1,\ldots,N\}$ denote the set of restoration agents. Because each agent observes only a local part of the PCPS and the recovery knowledge state summarizes time-dependent evidence history, we model restoration as a constrained decentralized partially observable Markov decision process (C-Dec-POMDP)
\begin{align}
\label{eq-cdecpomdp}
\mathcal{M}_{\rm rec}=\left\langle
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{X},\{\mathbb{U}_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}},
P,\{\mathcal{O}_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}},O,R,\mathcal{C},\gamma
\right\rangle,
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{X}$ is the latent cross-layer state space, $\mathbb{U}_i$ is the action space of agent $i$, $P(x_{t+1}\mid x_t,\mathbf{a}_t)$ is the state-transition kernel, $\mathcal{O}_i$ is the local observation space, $O$ is the observation model, $R$ is the cooperative restoration reward, $\mathcal{C}$ is the constraint set, and $\gamma\in(0,1)$ is the discount factor. We use $\mathbb{U}_i$ for action spaces to avoid overloading $\mathcal{A}_t$, which denotes dynamic KG assertions in Section~\ref{subsec-recovery-knowledge-plane}.

At time $t$, agent $i$ receives a local observation $o_{i,t}$ and has access to the task-relevant portion of the recovery belief state $\mathcal{B}_t$. Its decision information is written abstractly as
\begin{align}
\label{eq-information-state}
\iota_{i,t}=\big(h_{i,t},\mathcal{B}^{i}_t\big),
\end{align}
where $h_{i,t}=(o_{i,0},a_{i,0},\ldots,o_{i,t})$ is the local action--observation history and $\mathcal{B}^{i}_t\subseteq\mathcal{B}_t$ contains the recovery beliefs relevant to the agent's current task. The concrete extraction and encoding of $\mathcal{B}^{i}_t$ are implementation choices deferred to Section~V. A decentralized policy selects
\begin{align}
\label{eq-decentralized-policy}
a_{i,t}\sim\pi_i(\cdot\mid\iota_{i,t}),
\end{align}
and the joint candidate action is $\mathbf{a}_t=(a_{1,t},\ldots,a_{N,t})$.

The adversary affects decision making through admissible evidence streams within the budget $\Omega_A$ in Eq.~\ref{eq-attack-budget}. The observation and knowledge processes are attack dependent, while the physical transition kernel remains governed by the PCPS dynamics in Eq.~\ref{eq-system-transition}. The security problem is to keep corrupted observations from acquiring trusted recovery authority and influencing later control or learning.

\subsection{Risk-Aware Action Admissibility}
\label{subsec-risk-admissibility}
We partition the restoration constraints into invariant engineering constraints and evidence-dependent prerequisites,
\begin{align}
\label{eq-constraint-partition}
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\rm inv}\cup\mathcal{C}^{\rm evid}_t.
\end{align}
The set $\mathcal{C}^{\rm inv}$ contains requirements whose validity does not depend on online attack diagnosis, such as voltage and thermal limits, device ratings, power-balance relations, protection interlocks, and radiality. The set $\mathcal{C}^{\rm evid}_t$ contains runtime conditions inferred from evidence, such as controller availability, command-route reachability, or the trustworthiness of a measurement chain for a specific operation.

For a candidate joint action $\mathbf{a}$, let
\begin{align}
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{a})=\{q_1,\ldots,q_m\}
\end{align}
be the dynamic assertions that must hold for its cyber-physical execution. We assign each prerequisite a risk budget $\delta_q\in(0,1)$ and require
\begin{align}
\label{eq-belief-prerequisite}
b_t(q)\ge 1-\delta_q,
\qquad \forall q\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{a}).
\end{align}
The corresponding belief-admissible set is
\begin{align}
\label{eq-belief-admissible-set}
\mathbb{U}^{B}_t=
\left\{\mathbf{a}\ \middle|\
 b_t(q)\ge1-\delta_q,
\ \forall q\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{a})
\right\}.
\end{align}
Insufficient evidence does not create permission. The aggregate semantic risk budget for action $\mathbf{a}$ is
\begin{align}
\label{eq-semantic-risk-budget}
\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{a})}\delta_q\le\bar{\delta}_{B},
\end{align}
where $\bar{\delta}_{B}$ is selected according to the criticality of the restoration operation.

Invariant safety is checked independently of the belief gate. Let $\widehat{\mathcal{X}}_t$ be the conservative physical-state set defined under the trust boundary in Section~\ref{subsec-threat-model}, with $x_t\in\widehat{\mathcal X}_t$. We define
\begin{align}
\label{eq-invariant-admissible-set}
\mathbb{U}^{\rm inv}_t=
\Big\{\mathbf{a}\ \Big|\
&g_k(x,\mathbf{a})\le0,\ \forall k,\\
&h_l(x,\mathbf{a})=0,\ \forall l,
\ \forall x\in\widehat{\mathcal{X}}_t
\Big\}.\nonumber
\end{align}
The functions $g_k$ and $h_l$ represent inequality and equality/structural constraints, respectively. The final admissible action set is
\begin{align}
\label{eq-final-admissible-set}
\mathbb{U}^{\rm adm}_t
=\mathbb{U}^{B}_t\cap\mathbb{U}^{\rm inv}_t.
\end{align}
A learned surrogate may later reduce the number of candidates sent to exact verification, but it cannot enlarge $\mathbb{U}^{\rm inv}_t$ and has no independent authorization authority. If no nontrivial action is admissible, the controller executes a certified fallback action $\mathbf{a}^{\rm fb}_t\in\mathbb{U}^{\rm inv}_t$, such as maintaining isolation, retaining local control, or shedding a bounded amount of load.

\subsection{Robust Recovery Objective}
\label{subsec-robust-objective}
Let $\mathcal{L}$ be the set of loads, $P_l$ the nominal demand of load $l$, $w_l>0$ its service priority, and $y_{l,t}\in[0,1]$ its restored fraction at time $t$. The weighted restored-service ratio is
\begin{align}
\label{eq-weighted-recovery-ratio}
\eta_t=
\frac{\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}}w_lP_l y_{l,t}}
{\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}}w_lP_l}.
\end{align}
Safety is a hard constraint, so a large restoration reward cannot compensate for an unsafe operation. The stage reward captures service recovery and operational costs,
\begin{align}
\label{eq-robust-reward}
R_t=
\lambda_{\eta}\eta_t
-\lambda_{s}C^{\rm shed}_t
-\lambda_{o}C^{\rm op}_t
-\lambda_{c}C^{\rm cyber}_t
-\lambda_{\rm fb}C^{\rm fb}_t,
\end{align}
where $C^{\rm shed}_t$, $C^{\rm op}_t$, $C^{\rm cyber}_t$, and $C^{\rm fb}_t$ denote critical-load curtailment, switching/dispatch cost, cyber-resource recovery cost, and fallback-service loss, respectively.

Let $\mathfrak{A}(\Omega_A)$ denote the set of adversaries satisfying the capability and budget assumptions in Section~\ref{subsec-threat-model}. We seek a recovery policy and online belief-graph update rule that maximize worst-case expected recovery performance,
\begin{align}
\label{eq-minimax-objective}
\max_{\Pi,F_B}\ \inf_{\mathscr{A}\in\mathfrak{A}(\Omega_A)}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}
\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^t R_t\right],
\end{align}
where $\Pi=\{\pi_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}$ and
\begin{align}
\label{eq-belief-update-map}
F_B:(\mathcal G_t^B,\mathcal Z_{t+1})\mapsto\mathcal G_{t+1}^B
\end{align}
denotes the online recovery-belief update. The optimization in Eq.~\ref{eq-minimax-objective} is subject to
\begin{align}
\label{eq-execution-constraint}
\mathbf{a}^{\rm exec}_t\in\mathbb{U}^{\rm adm}_t,
\end{align}
and to the physical safety and recovery requirements
\begin{align}
\label{eq-safety-probability}
\Pr\left(x_t\in\mathcal{X}_{\rm unsafe}\right)&\le\delta_x,
\qquad 0\le t\le T,\\
\label{eq-recovery-liveness}
\Pr\left(\exists t\le T_{\max}:x_t\in\mathcal{X}_{\rm tar}\right)&\ge1-\delta_r.
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal{X}_{\rm unsafe}$ and $\mathcal{X}_{\rm tar}$ denote the unsafe and target recovery sets, respectively. Equation~\ref{eq-recovery-liveness} rules out a trivially safe controller that refuses every useful restoration action.

\subsection{Security Objectives: Bounding Knowledge Propagation}
\label{subsec-security-objectives}
We track propagation at four points in the recovery loop: the knowledge state, semantic action authority, post-attack correction, and the deployed policy. Section~V gives the corresponding mechanisms and Section~VI states sufficient conditions for the four bounds below.

\emph{1) Bounded belief distortion.}
Let $\mathcal{B}^{0}_t$ denote the counterfactual recovery belief state generated from uncompromised evidence and $\mathcal{B}^{A}_t$ the belief state under an admissible attack. To isolate knowledge-plane sensitivity, the comparison is conditioned on the same realized physical/action history up to time $t$; full closed-loop divergence is evaluated through the recovery objective and experiments. For the task-relevant assertion set $\mathcal{Q}_t$, define
\begin{align}
\label{eq-belief-distance}
D_B(\mathcal{B}^{A}_t,\mathcal{B}^{0}_t)
=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_t}\omega_q
\left|b^{A}_t(q)-b^{0}_t(q)\right|,
\qquad \sum_q\omega_q=1.
\end{align}
The first objective is
\begin{align}
\label{eq-bounded-belief-distortion}
\sup_{t\le T}
D_B(\mathcal{B}^{A}_t,\mathcal{B}^{0}_t)
\le \varepsilon_B(\Omega_A).
\end{align}

\emph{2) Bounded false semantic authorization.}
Define the ground-truth semantic-admissible set
\begin{align}
\label{eq-groundtruth-semantic-set}
\mathbb{U}^{B,\star}_t=
\left\{\mathbf{a}\ \middle|\
q_t^\star=1,\ \forall q\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{a})
\right\}.
\end{align}
Let $\mathbf{a}^{\rm sem}_t$ denote a candidate released by the semantic gate for invariant verification or an execution attempt. A false semantic authorization occurs when the belief gate admits this candidate even though at least one required runtime prerequisite is false. We require
\begin{align}
\label{eq-false-authorization-bound}
\Pr\left(
\mathbf{a}^{\rm sem}_t\in
\mathbb{U}^{B}_t\setminus\mathbb{U}^{B,\star}_t
\right)
\le \delta_{\rm auth}.
\end{align}
This event is upstream of invariant physical verification. Electrical and structural safety remain governed by $\mathbb{U}^{\rm inv}_t$, the authoritative verifier, and the trust assumption $x_t\in\widehat{\mathcal X}_t$.

\emph{3) Finite correction after poisoning.}
Suppose an attack affecting assertion $q$ ceases at time $t_a$ and sufficient uncompromised effective evidence resumes. For tolerance $\epsilon_{\rm corr}>0$, define the correction time
\begin{align}
\label{eq-correction-time}
T_{\rm corr}(q)=
\inf\left\{k\ge0:\left|b^{A}_{t_a+k}(q)-b^{0}_{t_a+k}(q)\right|\le\epsilon_{\rm corr}\right\}.
\end{align}
We seek conditions under which
\begin{align}
\label{eq-finite-correction}
T_{\rm corr}(q)\le\overline{T}_{\rm corr}
\end{align}
for all task-critical assertions whose trustworthy evidence becomes observable again.

\emph{4) Bounded knowledge-to-policy propagation.}
Let $\Pi^{A}_t$ and $\Pi^{0}_t$ denote online policies adapted from attacked and uncompromised recovery knowledge, respectively. To make cumulative policy drift comparable across promotion events, let $\mu^{\rm audit}$ be a fixed task-relevant audit distribution over information states and define
\begin{align}
\label{eq-policy-distance}
D_{\pi}(\Pi^{A}_t,\Pi^{0}_t)
=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\mathbb{E}_{\iota\sim\mu^{\rm audit}}
\left[
D_{\rm TV}
\left(
\pi^{A}_i(\cdot\mid\iota),
\pi^{0}_i(\cdot\mid\iota)
\right)
\right].
\end{align}
The required propagation bound is
\begin{align}
\label{eq-policy-propagation-bound}
D_{\pi}(\Pi^{A}_t,\Pi^{0}_t)
\le\varepsilon_{\pi}(\Omega_A).
\end{align}
Section~V enforces a KL trust region at each candidate-policy promotion, while Section~VI uses Pinsker's inequality on the fixed audit distribution to derive the deployed-policy bound in Eq.~\ref{eq-policy-propagation-bound}.

Together, Eqs.~\ref{eq-bounded-belief-distortion}--\ref{eq-policy-propagation-bound} specify the security problem used in the rest of the paper: bound error in the online recovery knowledge, keep that error from granting semantic authority to actions with false runtime prerequisites, correct poisoned beliefs after trustworthy evidence returns, and limit the resulting policy deviation.
