\section{CONCLUSION}
This paper presented KG-FedMARL, a knowledge-guided federated MARL framework for resilient restoration of power cyber-physical systems. The main argument is that cross-layer restoration needs a representation that can be queried, updated, and reused during control. KG-FedMARL therefore uses a dynamic power-CPS KG as the semantic state abstraction, the rule and dependency base for feasibility checking, and the memory of executed restoration outcomes. Fault and attack evidence is translated into timestamped graph operations rather than treated as an unspecified graph refresh.

The framework formulates restoration as a knowledge-conditioned constrained Markov game. A relation-aware graph encoder produces a task-subgraph state, decentralized role-specific agents propose local actions under a training-time critic, a three-level safety filter selects feasible commands, and block-wise asynchronous aggregation supports distributed training across heterogeneous clients. This design reduces the privacy-leakage risk associated with centralized transfer of raw operational traces, but it does not by itself provide a formal privacy guarantee. The appendix states the resulting safety and aggregation properties under explicit assumptions.

The experimental section specifies a standard evaluation path on IEEE 33-bus, 69-bus, 123-node, 8500-node, and regional-grid cases. It covers physical faults, DER outages, communication failures, FDI, DoS, protection misoperation, strong optimization and learning baselines, KG-specific ablations, safety-filter latency, communication overhead, convergence, and cross-scenario generalization. Quantitative claims about the incremental gain of KG-FedMARL should be finalized only after the reported result figures are regenerated from measured co-simulation logs.
