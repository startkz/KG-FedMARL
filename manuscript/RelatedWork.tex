\section{RELATED WORK}
\label{sec-related-work}
The literature relevant to this work spans three areas: recovery and resilient control under cyber-physical attacks, semantic knowledge representation for CPS operation, and poisoning attacks against knowledge-based learning. The representative literature reviewed below addresses these components mostly as separate problem classes. Our focus is narrower: the integrity of the runtime knowledge used to authorize recovery actions and to qualify subsequent online-learning updates.

\subsection{Recovery and Resilient Control under Cyber-Physical Attacks}
CPS recovery aims to return a compromised system to a target physical condition after an attack. Lu \emph{et al.} survey this area and distinguish recovery mechanisms according to whether a dedicated recovery controller is used, while also identifying diagnosis, attack history, and recovery-module assumptions as important open issues \cite{lu2024recovery}. Representative methods include real-time attack recovery based on linearized dynamics \cite{zhang2020real}, learning-assisted recovery from sensor attacks \cite{akowuah2021recovery}, and reinforcement-learning-assisted recovery of autonomous platforms \cite{fei2020learn}. Cross-layer resilience research further studies how cyber, communication, control, and physical dependencies shape defense and recovery decisions \cite{zhu2020cross}. Recent resilient-control work considers hybrid DoS/FDI attacks and multilayer coordination \cite{game2026hybrid,liao2025surveyresilient}, while representative power-distribution restoration work coordinates communication recovery with physical reconfiguration \cite{zhou2025faultresilient}.

Across the representative recovery methods above, measurements, diagnosis outputs, estimated states, and predefined cyber-physical constraints are inputs to recovery. Their primary guarantees concern state recovery, control feasibility, stability, or attack mitigation rather than a separate bound on the distortion and downstream propagation of a reusable recovery-knowledge state. This distinction motivates the knowledge-level threat model studied here.

\subsection{Dynamic Knowledge Representation for CPS Operation}
Knowledge graphs provide a structured representation for heterogeneous entities, relations, context, and rules, with support for querying, validation, inference, and graph-based learning \cite{hogan2021knowledgegraphs}. Research on the interaction between KGs and machine learning has likewise emphasized both knowledge-enhanced learning and learning-assisted knowledge refinement \cite{damato2023machine}. For CPSs, recent work has moved beyond static asset graphs. Gill \emph{et al.} represent time-continuous system behavior directly in KGs \cite{liu2025timecontinuous}; Ji \emph{et al.} use weighted KGs to combine formalized engineering knowledge with data-derived information dependencies \cite{wang2025weightedkg}; and Banerjee and Choppella use ontologies to support dynamic control-software reconfiguration \cite{knowledge2025dynamic}. In critical power infrastructure, Wu \emph{et al.} construct a heterogeneous cross-domain KG for threat modeling \cite{wu2026multidimensional}. More recently, Sankaranarayanan \emph{et al.} integrate cyber and physical grid information through a unified ontology-driven KG and demonstrate real-time cross-domain queries under structural updates \cite{sankaranarayanan2026ontology}.

These results show that operational KGs can evolve with system state and can expose cyber-physical dependencies to automated reasoning. A runtime assertion may nevertheless be semantically well formed and still be false because the evidence that created it was fabricated, replayed, delayed, correlated through a compromised upstream source, or selectively suppressed. The representative CPS-KG systems above focus on representation, interoperability, analysis, threat modeling, or reconfiguration. Their reported guarantees do not bound adversarial distortion of a recovery belief or connect such a belief to a quantified field-action authorization risk.

\subsection{Knowledge Poisoning and Knowledge-Guided Learning}
The security of knowledge representations has been studied mainly from the perspective of knowledge-graph embeddings. Zhang \emph{et al.} show that targeted additions and deletions of triples can manipulate KGE predictions \cite{zhang2019kgpoison}; Bhardwaj \emph{et al.} exploit relation-inference patterns to poison link-prediction models \cite{bhardwaj2021kgpoison}; and Zhou \emph{et al.} demonstrate poisoning of federated KGE through malicious local updates without directly modifying a victim's private KG \cite{zhou2024fkgepoison}. In these works, the attacked output is an embedding or link-prediction model; the papers do not study the distinct closed-loop setting considered here, where heterogeneous field evidence continually updates operational assertions that may authorize physical actions.

A separate line combines KGs with learning and control. The broader KG--ML literature treats symbolic knowledge as a source of constraints or context for data-driven models \cite{damato2023machine}, and recent industrial work combines KG retrieval with game-theoretic multi-agent learning for robust control design \cite{rodrigues2026agentic}. This line demonstrates semantic knowledge as a control-design input, but the cited framework is not designed around the replay-persistence problem studied here: a transition collected under contaminated recovery knowledge may continue to influence a deployed policy after the originating evidence has expired. Preventing this persistence requires explicit qualification of recovery transitions and bounded policy promotion, rather than only robust policy optimization.

\subsection{Positioning of This Work}
KG-FedMARL therefore does not claim novelty from constructing a dynamic KG, using MARL, applying federated learning, or placing a safety filter after a learned policy. Its contribution is the security boundary connecting these components. We model recovery knowledge as a versioned belief state inferred from potentially adversarial evidence, bound correlated evidence through provenance-aware influence limits, require uncertain prerequisites to pass a risk-aware authorization gate before exact physical verification, and prevent unverified recovery transitions from directly modifying the deployed policy. This yields an explicit chain from adversarial evidence to bounded belief distortion, bounded false authorization, finite post-attack correction, and bounded policy deviation. The representative recovery literature focuses on plant/controller recovery, the CPS-KG literature above focuses on representing or reasoning over operational knowledge, and the cited KG-poisoning work targets embedding or link-prediction integrity. This paper instead studies how corrupted operational knowledge propagates through a closed cyber-physical recovery loop.
