\section{RELATED WORK}
\label{sec-related-work}
Three bodies of work are most relevant: cyber-physical recovery, operational knowledge representation, and poisoning of knowledge-based models. Our concern lies at their intersection: runtime evidence is turned into recovery assertions, those assertions affect action authority, and the resulting transitions may later update the recovery policy.

\subsection{Recovery and Resilient Control under Cyber-Physical Attacks}
CPS recovery seeks to return a compromised system to an acceptable physical state after an attack. Lu \emph{et al.} survey recovery mechanisms and discuss assumptions about diagnosis, attack history, and the recovery module \cite{lu2024recovery}. Existing methods include real-time recovery based on linearized dynamics \cite{zhang2020real}, learning-assisted recovery from sensor attacks \cite{akowuah2021recovery}, and reinforcement-learning-assisted recovery of autonomous platforms \cite{fei2020learn}. Cross-layer resilience studies account for dependencies among cyber, communication, control, and physical components \cite{zhu2020cross}. Recent work also considers hybrid DoS/FDI attacks, multilayer coordination, and distribution-system restoration with communication recovery \cite{game2026hybrid,liao2025surveyresilient,zhou2025faultresilient}.

These methods use measurements, diagnosis outputs, estimated states, communication conditions, or predefined cyber-physical constraints to drive recovery. Their guarantees are usually stated in terms of state recovery, feasibility, stability, or attack mitigation. The integrity of a reusable recovery-knowledge state assembled from those inputs is less often treated as a separate security quantity.

\subsection{Dynamic Knowledge Representation for CPS Operation}
Knowledge graphs provide a structured representation of heterogeneous entities, relations, context, and rules, with support for querying, validation, inference, and graph learning \cite{hogan2021knowledgegraphs}. Work at the KG--machine-learning boundary has studied both knowledge-enhanced learning and learning-assisted KG refinement \cite{damato2023machine}. For CPSs, Gill \emph{et al.} represent time-continuous behavior in KGs \cite{liu2025timecontinuous}; Ji \emph{et al.} use weighted KGs for engineering knowledge and data-derived dependencies \cite{wang2025weightedkg}; Banerjee and Choppella use ontologies for dynamic control-software reconfiguration \cite{knowledge2025dynamic}; and Wu \emph{et al.} build a heterogeneous cross-domain KG for threat modeling in critical power infrastructure \cite{wu2026multidimensional}. Sankaranarayanan \emph{et al.} integrate cyber and physical grid information through an ontology-driven KG and report real-time cross-domain queries under structural updates \cite{sankaranarayanan2026ontology}.

This work establishes that operational KGs can change with system state and expose cyber-physical dependencies to automated reasoning. Semantic validity, however, is different from evidentiary validity. A well-formed assertion can still be false when it was created from fabricated, replayed, delayed, correlated, or selectively suppressed evidence. The CPS-KG systems above do not report a bound on that evidence-induced belief error or on the probability that such an error passes a recovery-action prerequisite.

\subsection{Knowledge Poisoning and Knowledge-Guided Learning}
Security studies of KGs have focused mainly on embeddings and link prediction. Zhang \emph{et al.} manipulate KGE predictions by adding or deleting triples \cite{zhang2019kgpoison}; Bhardwaj \emph{et al.} exploit relation-inference patterns to poison link-prediction models \cite{bhardwaj2021kgpoison}; and Zhou \emph{et al.} poison federated KGE through malicious local updates without direct access to a victim's private KG \cite{zhou2024fkgepoison}. Their attacked output is an embedding or prediction model. Our setting is different because field evidence continually changes operational assertions that can authorize a physical recovery action.

KGs have also been combined with learning and control. The broader KG--ML literature treats symbolic knowledge as a source of constraints or context for data-driven models \cite{damato2023machine}, and recent industrial work combines KG retrieval with game-theoretic multi-agent learning for robust control design \cite{rodrigues2026agentic}. A recovery transition creates an additional security issue: if it was collected under contaminated runtime knowledge, replay can preserve the effect of the original evidence after that evidence is no longer current. This motivates explicit transition qualification and bounded policy promotion.

\subsection{Positioning of This Work}
The contribution studied here is the integrity boundary between runtime evidence, recovery beliefs, action authorization, and online policy updates. Dynamic KGs, MARL, and federated training are implementation choices in KG-FedMARL. The security analysis instead concerns four propagation quantities: belief distortion, semantic false authorization, post-attack correction, and deployed-policy drift. This scope separates the paper from recovery methods whose inputs are treated as given, CPS-KG systems centered on representation and reasoning, and KG-poisoning work centered on embedding or link-prediction integrity.
